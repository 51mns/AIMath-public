## Canonical mathematical input

The public accepted AIMath proof gives, for every `k >= 0`,

\[
\mu(t_k)=\frac{p_k}{M_k},\qquad
r_k=M_k-p_k,
\]

and

\[
U_k=r_k-\frac{M_k}{5}.
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{U_k}{M_k}=1-\frac{p_k}{M_k}-\frac15
=\frac45-\frac{p_k}{M_k}.
\]

The same proof records `M_k \equiv 5 (mod 25)`, so `M_k/5` is integral.  Nothing below upgrades or alters that canonical claim.

## Primary-source placement

### 1. Drézet--Le Potier 1985: the classical exceptional-slope normalization

Primary source:

J.-M. Drézet and J. Le Potier, *Fibrés stables et fibrés exceptionnels sur P2*, Ann. Sci. École Norm. Sup. (4) 18 (1985), 193--243, DOI `10.24033/asens.1489`.

Source: https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_1985_4_18_2_193_0.pdf

On printed p.194 they define the slope of a rank-`r` bundle by

\[
\mu(E)=\frac{c_1(E)}{r}.
\]

They also state that an exceptional bundle is determined up to isomorphism by its slope and that its rank is the least positive denominator of the slope.

On printed p.195 they define the dyadic map `epsilon` whose image is exactly the exceptional slopes (Theorem A), and explicitly record

\[
\epsilon(-\rho)=-\epsilon(\rho),\qquad
\epsilon(\rho+n)=\epsilon(\rho)+n\quad(n\in\mathbb Z).
\]

Thus the natural classical slope symmetries visible in the original classification are sign and **integer** translation.

### 2. Veselov 2025: Markov fractions are exactly exceptional slopes

Primary source:

A. P. Veselov, *Markov fractions and the slopes of the exceptional bundles on P2*, arXiv:2501.06779v2 (2025).

Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.06779

In Section 3, Veselov defines

\[
\mu(E)=\frac{c_1(E)}{r(E)}
\]

and Theorem 3.1 states that the set of slopes of exceptional bundles on `P^2` coincides with the set of all Markov fractions.  Immediately after the theorem he notes that tensoring by `O(n)` and taking duals preserve exceptional bundles, so possible slopes are invariant under the integer affine group `Aff_1(Z)`.  Equivalently, the slope transformations generated by these standard operations are

\[
x\longmapsto \pm x+n,\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
\]

This is the same symmetry boundary already visible in Drézet--Le Potier's `epsilon` identities, but here it is stated directly for Markov fractions.

### 3. Exact geometric translation of the AIMath identity

By Veselov Theorem 3.1, for every source Markov fraction `p_k/M_k` there is an exceptional bundle `E_k` with

\[
\operatorname{rank}(E_k)=M_k,\qquad c_1(E_k)=p_k,
\]

because the slope is reduced and Drézet--Le Potier identify the rank with its least positive denominator.

Now apply only standard vector-bundle operations:

\[
F_k:=E_k^\vee\otimes O(1).
\]

For a rank-`M_k` bundle,

\[
c_1(E_k^\vee)=-p_k,
\qquad
c_1(E_k^\vee\otimes O(1))=-p_k+M_k.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mu(F_k)=\frac{M_k-p_k}{M_k}=\frac{r_k}{M_k}.
\]

Combining this source-backed geometric normalization with the canonical AIMath identity gives the exact placement

\[
\boxed{
\frac{U_k}{M_k}=\mu(E_k^\vee\otimes O(1))-\frac15.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
R_5(\mu(E_k))
=\frac45-\mu(E_k)
=\mu(E_k^\vee\otimes O(1))-\frac15.
}
\]

This is a useful geometric interpretation of the fixed-433 transformation.  It separates the relation into:

1. a completely standard exceptional-bundle symmetry `E -> E^vee tensor O(1)`, giving `x -> 1-x`;
2. a residual **non-integral slope translation** by `-1/5`.

The second step is exactly where the AIMath factor-5 arithmetic sits.

### 4. Why the residual `-1/5` is not an ordinary P2 line-bundle symmetry

Every composition of dualization and tensoring by an ordinary line bundle `O(n)` acts on slopes as

\[
x\mapsto \pm x+n,\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
\]

The fixed map

\[
R_5(x)=\frac45-x
\]

would require `n=4/5` in the negative-sign branch.  Hence it is not generated by those standard operations.  Likewise, after the standard dual-plus-twist step `x -> 1-x`, reaching `4/5-x` requires translation by `-1/5`, which is not tensoring by `O(n)` on ordinary `P^2`.

This is a source-normalization statement, not a novelty theorem: a nonstandard construction elsewhere in the literature could in principle produce the same rational shift.

### 5. Perling 2022: modular roots already have a Chern-class interpretation

Primary source:

M. Perling, *Unfocused notes on the Markoff equation and T-Singularities*, arXiv:2210.12982v1 (2022).

Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.12982

Section 3.1 defines Markoff weights `r_e,r_g,r_f` satisfying, for example,

\[
r_g^2\equiv -1\pmod g.
\]

Immediately before Lemma 3.1, Perling explicitly states that in Rudakov's work these weights were identified with first Chern classes of vector bundles in an exceptional collection on `P^2`, and the ratios `r_e/e`, `r_g/g`, `r_f/f` with their slopes.

This supplies an independent geometric placement for the familiar modular-root layer: square roots of `-1` modulo a Markoff denominator can occur as Chern numerators.  It does **not** by itself identify Perling's branch-oriented `r_g` with the AIMath representative `r_k=M_k-p_k` in every normalization.  The exact AIMath equality above instead follows safely from the accepted `r_k=M_k-p_k` together with the dual-plus-twist calculation.

## Result

**Classification: `BOUNDED_UNRESOLVED_WITH_EXCEPTIONAL_BUNDLE_PLACEMENT`.**

The exact prior-theory placement gained by this lane is:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{U_k}{M_k}
=\mu(E_k^\vee\otimes O(1))-\frac15,
\quad
\mu(E_k)=\frac{p_k}{M_k}.
}
\]

The `1-x` part is standard exceptional-bundle geometry; the remaining `-1/5` is outside the ordinary `Aff_1(Z)` slope symmetry generated by dualization and integral line-bundle twists.
